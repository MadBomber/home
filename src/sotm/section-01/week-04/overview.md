## The Big Picture

Treasure, sight, and service open this section as three ways of asking the
same question: what actually runs a life. A heart follows what it treasures
rather than the other way around, so the location of the treasure decides
everything downstream. Proverbs had already warned against wearing yourself
out to get rich (Proverbs 23:4-5) and had already prayed for neither poverty nor
riches, just enough (Proverbs 30:7-9), long before Jesus made the same case in three
short pictures: treasure, eye, and master.

The strongest Old Testament connection in the entire study sits in the
middle of this week. Exodus 16 gives Israel manna one day at a time, no
hoarding allowed, bread that rotted overnight if anyone tried to keep it
past its day. That is the same test Jesus describes when He tells a crowd
not to be anxious about tomorrow: daily bread, daily trust, no stockpile
required. A rescued people had to learn to depend on God one morning at a
time in the wilderness. This crowd is being asked to learn the same lesson
without the wilderness.

The section ends where wisdom literature already pointed: ask for the
kingdom first and let everything else follow, the way Solomon asked for
wisdom and received wealth and honor as well, unasked (1 Kings 3:5-14).
Anxiety and greed turn out to be the same problem wearing two faces, one
grasping forward and one grasping backward, and the cure offered for both
is the same reordering of what comes first.

## Where Luke Puts It

Every piece of this week survives in Luke, but none of them stay together.
Treasure and anxiety move to a single block on the road to Jerusalem
(Luke 12:22-34), the eye and the lamp turn up earlier in a saying about
spiritual sight (Luke 11:33-36), and two masters gets folded into a parable
about a dishonest manager (Luke 16:13). Seek first the kingdom keeps its
position as the section's closing line even after the move (Luke 12:31). Luke
has not lost any of Matthew's material here. He has simply let each piece
find its own place in a longer journey instead of holding them together as
one continuous teaching.

## Shown, Not Just Said

- **Where Your Treasure Is.** He had nowhere to lay His head by choice
  (Matthew 8:20), and the apostle who watched Him live that way later told a
  crippled beggar, "silver and gold I do not have" (Acts 3:6), and gave him
  something better instead.
- **The Eye Is the Lamp.** No clear scene demonstrates this one; the day
  carries the teaching without a matching narrative.
- **No One Can Serve Two Masters.** Offered every kingdom on earth for one
  act of worship, He refused outright (Matthew 4:8-10); Paul later refused
  support he was entitled to rather than let money cloud the message (Acts
  20:33-35).
- **Do Not Be Anxious.** Asleep on a cushion in a boat His own disciples
  thought was sinking (Mark 4:38); Paul and Silas sang hymns from a jail
  cell at midnight (Acts 16:25), and Peter slept between two guards the
  night before his scheduled execution (Acts 12:6).
- **Seek First the Kingdom.** "My food is to do the will of him who sent
  me" (John 4:34); the earliest church held what it had in common rather
  than in reserve (Acts 4:32-35).

## Key Themes

- **The Heart Follows the Treasure.** Not the other way around. Locate the
  treasure and the rest of a life becomes readable.
- **Manna Is the Pattern.** Exodus 16 taught daily dependence with no
  hoarding; this section asks for the same trust without a wilderness to
  force it.
- **Undivided Service.** Two masters is not a metaphor about balance. It is
  a statement that divided loyalty is not loyalty at all.
- **Anxiety and Greed, Same Root.** One grasps at what might run out, the
  other grasps at what might be lost; both get answered by the same
  reordering of priorities.
