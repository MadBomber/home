The characters in this page are figures in a story Jesus told -- but no fictional people have shaped more real lives. Stung by the Pharisees' grumble that "this man receives sinners and eats with them," Jesus answered with three lost things -- a sheep, a coin, and two sons (Luke 15).

The younger son's arc is conversion in miniature: the inheritance demanded (wishing his father dead, in effect), the far country, the famine, the pigsty where "he came to himself," and the rehearsed confession -- "I am no longer worthy to be called your son." He never gets to finish it. "While he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and *ran*" -- an old Middle Eastern landowner hiking his robes to sprint, absorbing the village's shame before his son could reach it -- "and embraced him and kissed him." Robe, ring, shoes, feast: sonship restored before the speech was done (Luke 15:11-24).

Then the parable turns to its real target. The elder brother -- dutiful, resentful, refusing to enter -- audits his years of service ("I never disobeyed your command") and disowns his sibling ("this son of yours"). The father comes out and pleads with him too: "Son, you are always with me... It was fitting to celebrate." There the story stops, mid-invitation, because its ending belonged to the Pharisees listening -- and to every older brother since. Both sons were lost; only one, so far, has come home.

## In the New Testament

- The parable of the two sons (Luke 15:11-32)
- Its frame: the lost sheep and the lost coin (Luke 15:1-10)
