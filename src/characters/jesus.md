Every other page on this site is a footnote to this one. John opens his Gospel above time itself -- "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" -- and then writes the sentence the whole Bible turns on: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:1, 14). The eternal Son entered his own creation through a Bethlehem feeding trough, grew up in an obscure Galilean town, worked with his hands, and for about three years taught, healed, forgave, stilled storms, and gathered a people.

The study's fifty-two weeks trace his path: born in Bethlehem, sheltered in Egypt, raised in Nazareth, baptized in the Jordan, tested in the wilderness, headquartered in Capernaum, transfigured on the mountain, and set toward Jerusalem "when the days drew near for him to be taken up" (Luke 9:51). There he was betrayed, tried, and crucified at Golgotha -- and on the third day the tomb in the garden was empty. "He is not here, for he has risen, as he said" (Matthew 28:6). Forty days later he ascended, and the rest of the New Testament is the story of his continuing work through his Spirit and his church.

No caption can hold him, and the study never tries: prophet, priest, king; Lamb of God and Lion of Judah; Son of Man and Son of God. The earliest confession remains the best summary of every week that follows: Jesus is Lord.

## In the New Testament

- The Word made flesh (John 1:1-18)
- Birth, boyhood, baptism, temptation (Luke 1-4; Matthew 1-4)
- The Galilean ministry (Matthew 4-18; Mark 1-9; Luke 4-9; John 1-6)
- The road to Jerusalem and Passion Week (Luke 9-23; John 11-19)
- Resurrection, appearances, ascension (Matthew 28; Luke 24; John 20-21; Acts 1)
- Reigning and returning (Philippians 2:5-11; Hebrews 1; Revelation 1, 19-22)
