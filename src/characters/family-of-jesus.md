Jesus grew up in a full house. Nazareth knew him as "the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon," with sisters besides (Mark 6:3). During his ministry that family appears in an uncomfortable scene: hearing reports that he was out of his mind, "his family... went out to seize him," and arrived at a packed Capernaum house asking for him. Jesus' answer redrew the map of kinship: "Whoever does the will of God, he is my brother and sister and mother" (Mark 3:21, 31-35). John adds the blunt note that "not even his brothers believed in him" (John 7:5).

The resurrection changed the family's mind. The risen Christ appeared personally to James (1 Corinthians 15:7); the brothers were praying with the disciples in the upper room before Pentecost (Acts 1:14); and two of them, James and Jude, went on to lead in the church and to write the letters that bear their names -- each introducing himself not as Jesus' brother but as his "servant" (James 1:1; Jude 1). The family that once came to bring him home ended up giving their lives to his mission.

## In the New Testament

- The brothers and sisters named at Nazareth (Mark 6:3)
- The family comes for him; kinship redefined (Mark 3:21, 31-35)
- The brothers' unbelief (John 7:1-10)
- After the resurrection: prayer, leadership, letters (Acts 1:14; 1 Corinthians 15:7; James; Jude)
