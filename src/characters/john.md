The younger Zebedee began as a Son of Thunder -- ready to burn Samaritan villages, jockeying for a throne, forbidding an outside exorcist because "he was not following us" (Luke 9:49-54; Mark 10:35-41). The Gospels never hide what grace had to work with. But John belonged to the inner three at the Transfiguration and Gethsemane, and in his own Gospel he takes a quieter title, never using his name: "the disciple whom Jesus loved."

That disciple leaned on Jesus' breast at the Last Supper, followed him into the high priest's courtyard, and stood at the cross when the rest had fled -- where Jesus, with nearly his last breath, gave him a mother: "Behold, your mother" (John 19:26-27). On resurrection morning he outran Peter to the tomb, saw the folded linens, "and believed" (John 20:1-10). In Acts he stands beside Peter at the Beautiful Gate healing and before the Sanhedrin -- the thunder now steady courage (Acts 3-4).

Tradition settles his long later life at Ephesus, shepherding the churches of Asia; exile took him to Patmos, "on account of the word of God" (Revelation 1:9). Five New Testament books bear his name: a Gospel written "that you may believe," three letters that circle one word -- the aged apostle, said the early church, was carried into meetings repeating "Little children, love one another" -- and the Revelation. The Son of Thunder became the apostle of love without losing the thunder: read his letters on truth and lies, and his vision of the Rider on the white horse.

## In the New Testament

- Called from the nets; the inner circle (Mark 1:19-20; 5:37; 9:2; 14:33)
- At the cross; entrusted with Mary (John 19:26-27)
- The empty tomb; the shore at dawn (John 20:1-10; 21:7, 20-24)
- With Peter in Acts (Acts 3-4; 8:14-25)
- His books: John; 1-3 John; Revelation
