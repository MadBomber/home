Twelve years of bleeding meant twelve years of ritual uncleanness: excluded from the temple, avoided by the scrupulous, and -- Mark adds with a physician's candor Luke tactfully softens -- she "had suffered much under many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better but rather grew worse" (Mark 5:26). Her plan was to steal a healing: slip through the crowd behind him, touch the fringe of his garment, and vanish. "If I touch even his garments, I will be made well."

The healing worked instantly; the anonymity did not. "Who touched my garments?" The disciples pointed out the absurdity -- the whole crowd was touching him -- but Jesus kept looking until, "in fear and trembling," she came and fell down and "told him the whole truth" (Mark 5:27-33). The exposure she dreaded became her blessing: he would not let her leave with a magical transaction when she could have a relationship. "Daughter" -- the only woman in the Gospels he addresses by that name -- "your faith has made you well; go in peace, and be healed of your disease."

An unclean woman touched him, and instead of defilement flowing to him, healing flowed to her -- the Gospels' physics of grace in a single gesture, painted on catacomb walls by Christians within living memory of the age of martyrs.

## In the New Testament

- The healing of the hemorrhaging woman (Mark 5:24-34; Matthew 9:20-22; Luke 8:42-48)
