Jesus' last teaching before the Passion is not a parable so much as a preview: "When the Son of Man comes in his glory... he will separate people one from another as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats" (Matthew 25:31-33). The judgment's evidence surprises both groups equally. To the sheep: "I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me."

Both the blessed and the condemned ask the same astonished question -- *Lord, when did we see you?* -- and the King's answer collapses the distance between himself and the overlooked: "as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" (Matthew 25:34-40). Mercy shown to the forgettable turns out to have been shown to the Judge himself; mercy withheld from them, withheld from him. The goats are not charged with atrocities, only with omissions -- the nothing they did.

The church has heard its marching orders here for two thousand years: every soup kitchen, hospital visit, prison ministry, and refugee welcome traces to these verses, where Christ identified his own face with the faces the world edits out.

## In the New Testament

- The sheep and the goats (Matthew 25:31-46)
- Its companions: mercy as the law's weight (Matthew 23:23; James 2:14-17; 1 John 3:17-18)
