Timothy grew up in Lystra with a believing Jewish mother, a Greek father, and a Bible-saturated childhood -- "from childhood you have been acquainted with the sacred writings," Paul reminded him, crediting "your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice" (2 Timothy 1:5; 3:15). He likely watched Paul stoned outside his own town; when the apostle returned, the young disciple with the good reputation joined the team (Acts 16:1-3), beginning the closest working friendship in the New Testament.

Paul's letters read like a file of references: "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord"; "I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare"; "as a son with a father he has served with me in the gospel" (1 Corinthians 4:17; Philippians 2:19-22). He was the apostle's shock absorber -- dispatched to steady Thessalonica, Corinth, and Philippi -- and co-sender of six letters. He was also, apparently, young, frequently ailing ("use a little wine for the sake of your stomach"), and temperamentally timid: "God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control" (1 Timothy 5:23; 2 Timothy 1:7). Grace built its most reliable courier out of fragile material.

Two canonical letters landed on his desk at Ephesus, where he held the hardest assignment on Paul's map; the last one is the dying apostle's farewell, asking Timothy to come before winter and bring the cloak and the books. Hebrews adds a final glimpse: "our brother Timothy has been released" (Hebrews 13:23) -- the timid one, prison-tested like his father in the faith.

## In the New Testament

- Recruited at Lystra (Acts 16:1-5)
- Troubleshooter of the churches (1 Corinthians 4:17; 16:10; Philippians 2:19-24; 1 Thessalonians 3:1-6)
- The letters to him: 1 and 2 Timothy
- His release (Hebrews 13:23)
