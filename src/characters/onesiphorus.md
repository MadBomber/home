By Paul's last imprisonment, association with him had become dangerous, and the ledger of 2 Timothy 1 records the result: "all who are in Asia turned away from me." Against that dark line stands one name in gold: "May the Lord grant mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains, but when he arrived in Rome he searched for me earnestly and found me" (2 Timothy 1:15-17).

Every verb tells. This was no chance visit: an Ephesian believer traveled to Nero's Rome -- after the fire, with Christians burning as scapegoats -- and went prison to prison asking after a condemned man, a search that itself invited arrest. He *found* Paul, and then came back "often," carrying the refreshment (the word suggests cool air on a stifling day) that the abandoned apostle never forgot. Paul adds that Ephesus already knew the man's quality: "you well know all the service he rendered at Ephesus" (2 Timothy 1:18).

Paul's prayer for him reaches forward -- "may the Lord grant him to find mercy from the Lord on that day" -- a deliberate echo: the man who searched and found will be found and rewarded. Onesiphorus stands in Scripture for the courage of showing up: the ministry, costly and unglamorous, of not being ashamed of somebody else's chains.

## In the New Testament

- The search and the refreshment (2 Timothy 1:15-18)
- Greetings to his household (2 Timothy 4:19)
