Pontius Pilate governed Judea for a decade (AD 26-36) -- attested by Josephus, Philo, Tacitus, and a stone bearing his name found at Caesarea -- and history remembers him for a single morning. The Gospels' portrait is of power without spine: he saw through the charges ("he knew it was out of envy that they had delivered him up"), declared three times "I find no guilt in him," and then bargained, deflected, and capitulated by stages -- Barabbas offered, Jesus scourged, the side-trip to Herod, the famous basin of water that cleaned nothing (Matthew 27:15-26; Luke 23; John 18-19).

His exchange with the prisoner is John's masterpiece of inverted judgment. "Are you the King of the Jews?" -- "My kingdom is not of this world." "So you are a king?" -- "For this purpose I was born... to bear witness to the truth." "What is truth?" said Pilate, with Truth standing in front of him (John 18:33-38). The crowd's final lever was the one his career could not survive: "If you release this man, you are not Caesar's friend" (John 19:12). He chose Caesar's friendship, seated himself at the judgment seat of Gabbatha, and handed over the Judge of the living and the dead.

He got two small last words: the titulus he refused to soften -- "What I have written I have written" -- and the guard he granted for the tomb, which succeeded only in certifying the resurrection's witnesses (John 19:19-22; Matthew 27:62-66). The creed fixed his name forever where he fixed the cross: "crucified under Pontius Pilate."

## In the New Testament

- The Roman trial (Matthew 27:11-26; Luke 23:1-25; John 18:28-19:16)
- The inscription and the burial permissions (John 19:19-22, 38; Matthew 27:57-66)
- Remembered in the church's preaching (Acts 3:13; 4:27; 1 Timothy 6:13)
