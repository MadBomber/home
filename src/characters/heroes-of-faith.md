Hebrews 11 is the Bible's portrait gallery, and its earliest frames hold the study's week-47 names. **Abel** heads the line: his acceptable sacrifice cost him his life at his brother's hand, "and through his faith, though he died, he still speaks" (Hebrews 11:4) -- the first martyr, whose blood cried from the ground until better blood spoke "a better word" (Hebrews 12:24). **Enoch** "walked with God, and he was not" (Genesis 5:24): taken without death, his whole biography compressed into the verse Hebrews hangs on him -- "without faith it is impossible to please God" (Hebrews 11:5-6). **Noah**, "warned by God concerning events as yet unseen," built a ship on dry land against a century of weather reports to the contrary, "and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith" (Hebrews 11:7).

Far down the chapter, past the patriarchs and Moses, stands the gallery's most surprising portrait: **Rahab**, the Canaanite prostitute of Jericho, who heard what everyone in her city heard and alone acted on it, hiding the spies and hanging the scarlet cord. "By faith Rahab... did not perish with those who were disobedient" (Hebrews 11:31) -- and she gained more than survival: a place in Israel, a marriage into Judah, and a line in the genealogy of the Messiah himself (Matthew 1:5; James 2:25).

A murdered shepherd, a man who skipped death, a builder of absurd obedience, a rescued outsider: the chapter's point is that the "cloud of witnesses" is not a hall of heroes but of believers -- and that the race they watched for is now ours to run, "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith" (Hebrews 12:1-2).

## In the New Testament

- Abel (Hebrews 11:4; 12:24; Matthew 23:35)
- Enoch (Hebrews 11:5-6; Jude 14)
- Noah (Hebrews 11:7; Matthew 24:37-39; 1 Peter 3:20; 2 Peter 2:5)
- Rahab (Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25; Matthew 1:5)
