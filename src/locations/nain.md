Nain was a small town on the northern slope of the hill of Moreh, at the edge of the Jezreel Valley -- a full day's walk of about 25 miles from Capernaum. It appears exactly once in Scripture, but the scene is unforgettable. As Jesus approached the town gate, a funeral procession was coming out: a young man, the only son of a widow, which in that world meant a woman left with no provider and no future.

Luke says Jesus "had compassion on her" and told her, "Do not weep" -- then touched the bier and spoke to the dead man: "Young man, I say to you, arise" (Luke 7:11-17). The crowd's reaction reaches back centuries: "A great prophet has arisen among us!" They knew their history. Just over the same hill, at Shunem, Elisha had raised another widow's son (2 Kings 4). The location was the sermon: the God who worked through the prophets was at work again, in person.

The Arab village of Nein still occupies the site, with a small Franciscan church commemorating the miracle.

## In the New Testament

- The raising of the widow's son (Luke 7:11-17)
- Echoes of Elijah and Elisha raising widows' sons nearby (1 Kings 17:17-24; 2 Kings 4:18-37)
