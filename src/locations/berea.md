Berea was a quieter town in the foothills southwest of Thessalonica, off the main highway -- which is partly why Paul was sent there by night when Thessalonica turned dangerous. What he found in its synagogue earned the town a permanent adjective. "Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so" (Acts 17:11).

Luke's praise is carefully double-edged: eagerness *and* examination. The Bereans neither dismissed the new teaching nor swallowed it; they took Paul's claims back to the text, every day, and "many of them therefore believed." Ever since, "Berean" has been shorthand for the conviction this study site is built on -- that the right response to any teacher is an open Bible.

Agitators from Thessalonica eventually tracked Paul down and stirred the crowds, and the brothers spirited him off to the coast and on to Athens; Silas and Timothy stayed to ground the young church (Acts 17:13-15). Berea contributed Sopater to Paul's later traveling team (Acts 20:4), and modern Veria still marks the traditional preaching spot with the mosaic-covered "Bema of the Apostle Paul."

## In the New Testament

- The noble Bereans (Acts 17:10-15)
- Sopater the Berean accompanies Paul (Acts 20:4)
